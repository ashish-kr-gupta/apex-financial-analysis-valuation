# Apex Consumer Electronics — Financial Analysis & Valuation

## Project Overview

This project presents an end-to-end financial analysis and valuation model for Apex Consumer Electronics Pvt. Ltd., a fictional company created for financial modelling practice.

The objective is to analyse historical financial performance, develop a driver-based forecast, connect the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, estimate free cash flow to the firm (FCFF), and calculate a discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation.

The model also includes operating scenario analysis and WACC–terminal growth sensitivity analysis to assess how changes in assumptions affect estimated equity value.

> **Disclaimer:** Apex Consumer Electronics is fictional. The financial figures and valuation outputs are for educational and portfolio demonstration purposes only and do not represent a real company's financial position or investment advice.

## Objectives

- Analyse historical revenue, profitability, liquidity and leverage.
- Evaluate working-capital efficiency and the cash conversion cycle.
- Forecast financial performance using operating assumptions.
- Build integrated three-statement projections.
- Calculate FCFF and weighted average cost of capital (WACC).
- Estimate enterprise value and equity value using DCF.
- Evaluate operating scenarios and valuation sensitivity.
- Communicate findings and recommendations in an analyst-style report.

## Tools & Skills

- **Power BI:** financial KPI visualisation and dashboard reporting, where applicable.
- **Financial analysis:** profitability, liquidity, leverage and working-capital ratios.
- **Valuation:** FCFF, CAPM, WACC, terminal value and DCF.

## Methodology

1. **Data preparation:** clean and structure the financial dataset.
2. **Historical analysis:** evaluate revenue, margins, returns, liquidity, leverage and working capital.
3. **Forecast assumptions:** set revenue growth, margin, tax, working-capital and CapEx assumptions.
4. **Financial modelling:** project the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement.
5. **FCFF analysis:** calculate operating cash flow available to debt and equity capital providers after reinvestment.
6. **DCF valuation:** discount forecast FCFF using WACC and estimate terminal value.
7. **Scenario and sensitivity analysis:** test alternative operating assumptions and combinations of WACC and terminal growth.
8. **Reporting:** summarise findings, limitations and potential management actions.

## Selected Findings

- Historical revenue increased from ₹92 crore in FY2024 to ₹128 crore in FY2026.
- FY2026 revenue growth was approximately 18.52%.
- FY2026 EBITDA was ₹23.2 crore, with an EBITDA margin of approximately 18.13%.
- Historical cash conversion cycle was approximately 87.45 days in FY2026.
- Base-case DCF estimated equity value was ₹83.41 crore.
- Operating scenario outputs were ₹55.67 crore for the downside case, ₹83.41 crore for the base case and ₹104.51 crore for the upside case.

These outputs are model-dependent estimates. They should be treated as final only after the workbook's formulas, statement linkages and sensitivity outputs have been validated.

## Key Analytical Takeaways

- Revenue growth should be assessed alongside margins and cash generation.
- Working-capital requirements can absorb cash as sales increase.
- FCFF reflects operating cash generation after capital expenditure and changes in operating working capital.
- DCF valuation depends on forecast cash flows, WACC and terminal growth assumptions.
- Scenario and sensitivity analysis help illustrate valuation uncertainty rather than establish a guaranteed market value.
